# Water Quality Testing Guide

This guide explains how AquaPure technicians and customers should test water, interpret results, and decide whether a purifier is working correctly. It is not a medical or regulatory document.

## What to Test

At minimum, test total dissolved solids (TDS), pH, and turbidity. Where there is a concern about hardness, iron, chlorine, nitrate, fluoride, or microbial contamination, use a certified laboratory test. A TDS meter is a screening tool, not a complete water-safety test.

## TDS Interpretation

| TDS (ppm) | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Below 50 | Very low mineral content; taste may be flat |
| 50 to 300 | Generally good for drinking |
| 300 to 500 | Acceptable, but RO is often preferred |
| 500 to 1,000 | Use RO; test hardness and other parameters |
| Above 1,000 | Not recommended without treatment; consider commercial RO |

TDS does not measure bacteria, viruses, nitrate, arsenic, or fluoride. A low TDS does not prove that water is safe.

## pH and Other Parameters

Drinking water is typically between pH 6.5 and 8.5. Low pH can corrode plumbing; high pH can cause scaling. Hardness above 300 mg/L as calcium carbonate may cause scale and shorten membrane life. Chlorine should be low at the point of use; carbon filtration removes it. Iron above 0.3 mg/L can stain fixtures and foul membranes.

## Testing Procedure

1. Collect a sample in a clean glass or food-grade plastic container. Do not use a bottle that contained soap or chemicals.
2. Rinse the container three times with the water being tested.
3. For inlet TDS, collect water before the purifier. For outlet TDS, run the tap for 30 seconds and collect treated water.
4. Rinse the TDS meter probe with distilled water, immerse it, and wait for the reading to stabilise.
5. Record inlet TDS, outlet TDS, pH, date, and source.
6. Calculate rejection: rejection (%) = (inlet TDS - outlet TDS) / inlet TDS x 100.

## RO Performance

A healthy RO membrane should reject at least 85% of inlet TDS under normal conditions. Rejection below 85%, or a rising outlet TDS trend, suggests the membrane is exhausted, the pre-filter is clogged, or the inlet pressure is low. Check the pre-filter and pressure before replacing the membrane. Rejection below 75% usually requires membrane replacement.

## Testing Frequency

Residential systems should be tested every 6 months and after every filter or membrane change. Commercial systems should be tested monthly at the inlet and outlet, with a full laboratory panel at least quarterly. Whole-house systems should be tested after any muddy-water event or plumbing repair.

## When to Recommend a Different Product

Recommend RO when TDS is above 500 ppm, hardness is high, or specific dissolved contaminants are present. Recommend UV only for clear water with low TDS and a safe microbiological source. Recommend whole-house pre-treatment when sediment or pressure problems affect multiple taps. Recommend a laboratory test and independent treatment design when arsenic, fluoride, nitrate, or microbial contamination is suspected.

## Record Keeping

Keep test results with the service ticket. Trends matter more than a single reading. If a customer disputes water quality, compare inlet and outlet results, confirm the sampling method, and escalate to the technical desk.
